What this is

A body is a tool, an identity is a key

Parazit is a third-person action/stealth game about a bodiless consciousness that survives by jumping between living bodies in the middle of a fight. Every body you take gives you its identity, its access, its weapon and its function — along with its limits.

The core loop is not simply changing bodies; it is reading what each body is good for and spending it as a temporary resource.

How it plays

Three rules

Move quietly. Take the right body. Then live with what you left behind.

Jump

Take a body within range. The shell you leave behind drops where it stands and stays there as evidence — who fell, and where, is a trail for whoever is looking for you.

Read

Every body opens a different door. A technician's clearance, a guard's weapon, the room a nurse can walk into. Being in the wrong place in the wrong body is how you get noticed.

Spend

Shells run out. The body you stay in wears down, gets recognised, gets hunted. Knowing when to leave matters as much as knowing where to go next.

The story

What remains of a person

In Karya, taking another body is the easy part. Being seen doing it is what ends you.

A failed consciousness-transfer experiment kills Subject 07. His mind survives. Now known only as Misafir — the Guest — he escapes into Karya, a near-future city where the wealthy are attempting to purchase continuity beyond death, while the poor increasingly survive by selling access to their own bodies.

Every transfer leaves two problems behind. The shell he abandons drops where it stands and stays there as evidence. And if someone watched that face change, nobody trusts it again — including the people who loved it.

Guided by a woman searching for her missing brother and hunted by the corporation that created him, Misafir begins uncovering fragments suggesting that the life he is trying to recover may never have belonged entirely to him.

And somewhere in Karya, another consciousness has already learned how to live without a permanent body.

Parazit is ultimately not about changing bodies. It is about what remains of a person when the body, the face and even the memories can no longer prove who they are.
